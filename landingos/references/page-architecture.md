# Page architecture: the order of the sections

A LandingOS sales page is long on purpose: every question a cold visitor has gets its own screen. The order follows the copy, never a template, but the arc is the same.

## The arc

1. **Hook and a trust signal in the first screen**: press belt or brand mark, the promise, one button.
2. **Feel the problem**, told with scale and motion, not paragraphs.
3. **Credibility early**: a proof strip or the authority stack, before asking for anything big.
4. **Reframe**: the number, the gap, what does not work.
5. **Reveal** the product as the hero object of the page.
6. **How it works**: route, capability list, mechanism.
7. **For whom**, with a not-for card. Excluding someone builds trust.
8. **Proof wall** right before money.
9. **Money**: anchor (what it costs elsewhere), value list, coupon. The button repeats after every money moment.
10. **Objections**: guarantee, FAQ.
11. **Final**: price again, face again, one field.

## Two ways to run the arc

- **One-time offer** (course, product, workshop): arc as written. Authority comes early, because the reader is buying one decision from one person.
- **Ongoing offer** (membership, retainer, tool): move outside urgency and the promise up front, repeat the form two or three times down the page, and move the founder's story toward the end, where it answers "who will be there next month?".

## Placement rules

Buttons appear at the hero, after the product reveal, on every price sheet or form, and at the end. Never in the middle of the story.
Breathing: after every 2 dense sections, one sparse one (single statement, giant type, short list).
Colour: 2-3 full colour sheets per page, spaced apart. The last one is the closing sheet.
