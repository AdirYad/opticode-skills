# Why a story works

Stories reach people who already follow the account, so this is a tool for deepening a relationship. Finding new people is another channel's job. Three questions run this file: will the story be seen, what does it do for the viewer's trust, and what happens next.

## 1. Will it be seen?
The row of rings at the top of the app is ordered per viewer, and the accounts that person interacts with come first. Two habits move an account forward in that row:
- **A reason to respond, every day.** A reply is the strongest signal a viewer can give. A day without a question or a reply word earns almost none.
- **Showing up without gaps.** Skipped days cost position, and that position takes a while to win back.

Practical consequences:
- Text frames get tapped through and read. Video builds warmth but loses more people. Unless there's a reason otherwise, the day opens on a text frame.
- Attention falls with every frame, so the point of the day lives in the first two.
- A free thing offered for a reply word produces the biggest days. Use it about twice a week, more often and it stops feeling like a treat.
- The profile picture appears on every ring and in every DM: a close-up that can be recognised at thumbnail size.

## 2. What does it do for trust?
Before anyone pays, four doubts have to go quiet. Each story should clearly work on one of them. Too many days on the same doubt is what makes an account feel flat.

| the doubt | what quiets it | day kind |
|---|---|---|
| "Is there a real person here?" | the daily life around the work, an honest bad week, the road so far | know |
| "Do they know what they're talking about?" | a firm opinion, a popular belief taken apart, a hard idea made simple | rethink |
| "Does it work for someone like me?" | real messages, measured results, the work seen while it's happening | proof |
| "Is it worth it for me, right now?" | the cost of waiting, who it isn't for, what removes the risk | decide |

Two more day kinds feed those: **answer** (the viewer says something, and that alone builds the habit) and **take** (the viewer gets something useful for a word).

A feed that only teaches gets followers who don't care who's teaching. A feed that only shares life gets friends who don't buy. Mix them. Proof works best on screen, so a sales frame leans on a real message or a real number. If the result is a feeling, turn it into something countable: hours, shekels, phone calls.

Roughly one day in ten sells directly. The other nine earn it.

## 3. Selling across frames
One frame can't sell anything larger than a reply. A series works because each frame answers what the viewer is silently asking after the one before:

1. Why should I stop tapping?
2. Is this about me?
3. What's different from what I've already tried?
4. Who did it work for, and were they like me?
5. What exactly do I get?
6. What happens if I leave it?
7. What do I do now?

Each frame ends on the question the next one answers, so stopping feels like leaving in the middle of a sentence. What changes in the viewer's life is the story. The inner workings are for the sales page. After the ask, a frame or two more of proof catches people who didn't act the first time.

## 4. The first frame
- Start at the outcome or at the loss. Background comes later or not at all.
- Exact beats round: a specific figure, a specific span of time.
- Borrow what has already worked. The buyer's own best openings first, then shapes that are working in the field, filled with today's material.
- It has one job: earning the next two seconds. Every word that doesn't help comes out.
- The audience's vocabulary, never the profession's.

## 5. The ask
- One thing to do and one place it goes: `תגיבו "מילה"`. Options multiply hesitation.
- An organic ask offers something free or cheap. Something expensive needs a series and a conversation in DM.
- Someone who just replied is at their most interested. The message back goes out within seconds (`references/reply-automation.md`).

## 6. The daily habit
The buyer has minutes, not a creative department. So the skill hands over **a finished assignment rather than a question**: the words of each frame, what to shoot or which file to use, the reply word, the time, and one line on why it should work. `stories/ideas.md` holds ten or more unused ideas at all times.

A daily story combines three things: a day kind, a format from `references/formats.md`, and one piece of real material from the business, framed around the result the client pays for. Without the third it becomes generic and can be skipped.

## 7. Highlights
Four or five at most, for example who this is, how it works, results, a look inside. Frames meant for highlights carry no date and no "הערב".
