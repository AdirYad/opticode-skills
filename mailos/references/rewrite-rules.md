# Drafting and repair

Nothing here may add a fact. A draft is built backwards, from what the reader should believe at the end, because an email written from its first line tends to wander.

## Drafting order
1. **The belief.** One sentence, kept out of the email: what the reader should think after finishing that they did not think before. Two beliefs means two emails.
2. **The evidence.** Pick one or two ledger rows that make the belief hard to refuse. No row supports it? Choose a different belief, or ask the buyer.
3. **The ask.** What the reader should do next, and what truly happens when they do. A value email may ask for nothing but a reply.
4. **The way in.** The reader knows something the belief contradicts, so start there. Open mid-scene and leave the background for later. Good entries: an hour and a place, something the audience says to themselves, a mistake from the ledger, a ledger number that looks wrong at first glance.
5. **The turn.** One place where the reader's view shifts. Everything before it builds toward it and everything after it cashes it in. Put the sentence that carries the turn in bold. Launch emails put a link in the first paragraph too, since many readers have already decided.
6. **The P.S.** Many readers jump there first. It gives a second reason, separate from the body: a different objection, the true deadline, a benefit the body skipped.

Read it aloud. Any sentence that would sound strange said to a friend gets rewritten.

Hold at most one open question across the email and close it before the end. A question that carries over to the next email is fine only when that email really exists in the sequence. At most one comparison from everyday life, and a made-up example has to be visibly made up.

## Repairs
After the draft, diagnose it and apply **at most two** repairs from this table, the two that hurt most. Every repair ends with this check: **לא נוסף שום מספר, שם, אירוע או ציטוט שאין בפנקס.**

| symptom | repair |
|---|---|
| The first paragraph could open any email | Replace it with a specific moment from a ledger story, or an audience sentence from the ledger |
| It reads like a summary | Keep only the part with tension, cut a third of the words |
| Abstract words, nothing to picture | Swap the category for one physical detail the ledger has |
| The reader finishes with nothing to use | Add one thing to do today, from what the product or the ledger already teaches |
| Everyone already agrees with it | State the common fix from the ledger's existing solutions and show where it fails |
| It sounds like an announcement | Retell it through one story from the ledger, or a parable labelled as one |
| The reader's own voice is missing | Open a section with a real audience quote from the ledger |
| A paragraph has no point | Ask what changes if it's gone. Nothing? Delete it |
| Too much "אני" and "זה" | Replace with the real noun or remove |

## Never
Instructions like "add urgency" or "add research and statistics": the model will produce them from nothing. Stories about clients who don't exist. Questions the seller wrote, presented as if the audience asked them. Warnings that the price is about to rise.
